# Feature Video Scoring Rubric

**Version:** 1.0
**Purpose:** Reusable Keep/Edit/Rebuild rubric for feature videos in any subject area, including math, STEM, ELA, sports/movement, humanities, and interdisciplinary work.

## How to use

Score the finished video—not the brief, intention, or raw assets. Review the complete video once for overall experience, then review it again with pauses for evidence.

For every score, record at least one timecode, scene, frame, caption, or quoted line. Separate content defects from production defects and mark each issue as blocker, major, or minor. Score the universal core first, then add subject-specific modules when needed. A high average cannot hide a blocker.

## Five-level scale

| Level | Name | Meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| 5 | Exceptional | Clear, accurate, purposeful, audience-fit, and publication-ready. |
| 4 | Strong | Works well and needs only minor polish or limited omissions. |
| 3 | Adequate | Functional and understandable but uneven or missing meaningful refinement. |
| 2 | Weak | Important confusion, omission, pacing, accessibility, or production defects reduce value. |
| 1 | Failing | Incorrect, misleading, unusable, inaccessible, structurally broken, or fundamentally mismatched. |

Half-points may be used only when the evidence is explained; whole numbers are preferred.

## Universal ten-criterion core — 50 points

### 1. Intent, audience, and promise

Does the video deliver the experience, learning outcome, or story promised by its title, opening, brief, and target audience? Check purpose, opening stakes, age/cultural fit, prior knowledge, and closing payoff.

### 2. Subject accuracy and integrity

Are claims, examples, demonstrations, interpretations, numbers, quotations, and conclusions correct and honestly represented? Check factual errors, distinctions between fact and speculation, required attribution, and misleading framing.

### 3. Depth, value, and insight

Does the feature format have enough substance? Look for a meaningful idea, useful context, mechanisms, examples, comparisons, consequences, and a level of depth appropriate to the audience and runtime.

### 4. Narrative and structural clarity

Can the viewer follow why each section exists and how it advances the whole? Check establish → develop → explain/complicate → resolve/payoff progression, signposting, logical steps, transitions, and ending.

### 5. Explanation, demonstration, and evidence alignment

Do narration, examples, demonstrations, graphics, and evidence support the same idea at the same time? Check paired narration/visual timecodes and whether examples actually illuminate the claim.

### 6. Visual communication and art direction

Do composition, hierarchy, color, typography, motion, and image selection make the subject easier to understand? Check consistency, useful visual variety, cropping, noise, and generated-media artifacts.

### 7. Audio, voice, music, and caption quality

Is the audio intelligible, balanced, intentional, and synchronized? Check voice clarity, levels, edits, clipping, silence, captions matching spoken words/timing/punctuation/terminology, and whether music/effects support rather than compete.

### 8. Pacing, attention, and emotional rhythm

Does the video give enough time to perceive, think, feel, and act without dragging or rushing? Check shot/slide duration, breathing room, pace variation, dead air, repetition, rushed captions, and platform-appropriate rhythm.

### 9. Accessibility, legibility, and platform fit

Can the intended audience access important content on the intended device and platform? Check text size, contrast, line length, safe margins, captions or equivalent access, color dependence, aspect ratio, crop, and mobile readability.

### 10. Technical, editorial, and delivery quality

Is the export stable and ready for publication or the next workflow stage? Check resolution, frame rate, aspect ratio, duration, codec/container, black or frozen frames, missing media, accidental overlays, placeholders, watermarks, file-path leakage, clean beginning/end, filename, audio, captions, and package completeness.

## Decision rules

### Keep

Use **Keep** when the video is ready for the next workflow stage or publication:

- 40–50 points;
- no universal criterion below 3;
- no blocker; and
- subject-specific accuracy is acceptable.

### Edit

Use **Edit** when the core concept, footage/assets, and structure are worth preserving but targeted changes are needed. Typical conditions are 25–39 points, one or more criteria at 2, or a major localized defect that does not require replacing most of the video.

Every Edit decision must name the scene/timecode, problem, severity, exact correction, acceptance test, and what should be preserved.

### Rebuild

Use **Rebuild** when the foundation is not trustworthy or changes affect most of the video. Typical conditions are 10–24 points, a 1 in subject accuracy/narrative/accessibility/technical delivery, a conclusion-changing factual error, substantial missing or incorrect audio/visuals, or failure to deliver the stated purpose.

### Override rule

A critical defect overrides the numerical average. A video with a high visual score but incorrect content is not Keep-ready.

## Evidence and issue log

Use this format for every meaningful issue:

```markdown
### Issue 01
- Timecode/scene/frame: 00:00–00:00 or Scene 03
- Type: content | production | accessibility | technical
- Severity: blocker | major | minor
- Criterion: 1–10 or subject module
- Observation: What is visibly/audibly wrong or missing?
- Impact: How does it affect understanding, trust, access, or delivery?
- Action: The exact edit or correction required.
- Acceptance test: What must be true after the revision?
- Preserve: Assets, narration, timing, layout, or other elements to keep.
```

Avoid comments such as “make it better,” “fix pacing,” or “improve visuals” without a location and acceptance test.
